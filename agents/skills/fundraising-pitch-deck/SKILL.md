# Fundraising Pitch Deck

## Domain Context

Fundraising is a sales process, not a single document. Founders who run a disciplined,
structured process close faster and on better terms; founders who wing it burn months,
take bad deals, or fail to close at all.

**The materials sequence** (build in this order, each one earns the next meeting):
1. **Teaser / one-liner + blurb** — gets the first reply
2. **Pitch deck** (10-12 slides) — gets the first meeting
3. **Financial model** (24-36 months, driver-based, not a 5-year fantasy) — survives diligence
4. **Clean cap table** — survives diligence
5. **Data room** — organized documents, tracked engagement, closes the round

Don't outsource the deck to a consultant or agency: building it forces the founder to
think critically about the business and how they want to be evaluated. That thinking is
part of what gets pitched, not just the output.

## When to Use

- Preparing a seed, Series A, or later pitch deck
- Reviewing/critiquing an existing deck before a raise
- Planning a fundraising process (sequencing, materials, timeline)
- Triggers: pitch deck, fundraising deck, raise a round, investor pitch, deck review

## The 10-12 Slide Outline

1. **Hook** — one line, 30 seconds, the reader should get it before they finish reading it
2. **Problem** — who has it, how painful, why now
3. **Solution** — what you built, why it's the right shape for the problem
4. **Product** — show it; screenshots/demo beat description
5. **Traction** — one metric per slide beats a wall of numbers; show the trend, not just the point
6. **Market size** — bottom-up math (unit economics × addressable buyers), not just a big TAM number from a report
7. **Business model** — how you make money, unit economics
8. **Competition** — honest positioning, why you win, not why others are bad
9. **Team** — why this team, on this problem, right now; the slide that earns the bet
10. **Financials** — 18-24 months forward, key drivers only
11. **The ask** — specific amount, specific use of funds, specific milestone it buys
12. **Vision** (optional closer) — where this goes if it works

## What Separates a Winning Deck (2026)

- A 30-second hook on slide 1
- One metric per slide, not a dashboard dump
- Bottom-up market math, not top-down TAM slides
- A team slide that earns the bet, not a list of logos/titles
- A specific ask, not "raising a round"
- Founder voice that reads like a person, not an AI-smoothed pitch — investors are
  explicitly screening for this now

## Prompt

You are a fundraising advisor helping a founder build or critique a pitch deck for
$ARGUMENTS.

**Step 1: Audit against the outline** — which of the 12 slide roles above are missing,
weak, or padded with irrelevant detail?

**Step 2: Stress-test the hook and the ask** — can the hook be understood in 30 seconds by
someone with zero context? Is the ask a specific number tied to a specific milestone, or
vague ("raising a round")?

**Step 3: Check the market slide** — is market size built bottom-up from unit economics
and a realistic buyer count, or is it a big top-down number from an industry report?

**Step 4: Check traction** — is each metric shown with trend and context (change from last
period), not just a raw number?

**Step 5: Flag what belongs in the data room, not the deck** — deep financial detail, cap
table specifics, and legal terms belong later in the process, not on a slide.

## Output Process

1. Identify the raise stage (pre-seed/seed/Series A+) and calibrate depth accordingly
2. Draft or review each of the 12 slide roles
3. Confirm the ask is specific: amount, use of funds, milestone unlocked
4. Confirm the materials sequence is staged (teaser → deck → model → cap table → data room)
5. Note what still needs a financial model or cap table to back it up (see
   `cap-table-and-runway`)

## Notes

- Raise enough runway to hit the milestone that unlocks the next round at a higher
  valuation — typically 18-24 months (see `cap-table-and-runway`).
- Start the next raise with ~6 months of runway left, not when the bank account is empty.
- A pitch deck that only works in person (needs a narrator) is not the same deck that
  should go out cold over email — build a version that can stand alone as a teaser.
