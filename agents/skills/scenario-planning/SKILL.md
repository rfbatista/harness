# Scenario Planning

## Domain Context

Scenario planning develops strategies that work across several plausible futures rather
than betting everything on one prediction. Formalized in the 1990s by Global Business
Network (building on scenario work pioneered at Shell), the core technique is the 2x2
scenario matrix built from the two most important, most uncertain driving forces facing
the business.

The output isn't a forecast — it's a set of distinct future worlds the strategy needs to
survive in, so the plan can be pressure-tested before commitment rather than after reality
picks a scenario for you.

## When to Use

- The environment is too uncertain for a confident single-point forecast
- About to commit to a strategy that only makes sense if one specific future happens
- Long-range planning (typically a 5-10 year horizon) under real uncertainty
- Triggers: scenario planning, 2x2 scenario matrix, future scenarios, plausible futures,
  strategic foresight

## The Process

1. **Define the focal question** — the specific strategic decision or challenge this
   exercise needs to inform (e.g. "should we enter market X in the next 3 years?"), with a
   time horizon.

2. **Identify driving forces** — brainstorm external factors (political, economic, social,
   technological, environmental — see `pestle-analysis`) that could shape that future.

3. **Rank by impact and uncertainty** — plot driving forces on an impact/uncertainty 2x2.
   High-impact, high-uncertainty forces are the **critical uncertainties**; low-uncertainty
   forces are treated as "predetermined" background, not scenario axes.

4. **Select the two most critical uncertainties** to form the scenario axes — the two
   factors that are both highest-impact and most genuinely unpredictable.

5. **Define the axis endpoints** — each axis is a spectrum between two plausible extremes,
   not a single prediction.

6. **Develop scenario narratives** — each of the four quadrants becomes a distinct,
   internally consistent story of how the world looks, incorporating the other
   (non-axis) driving forces and trends identified in step 2.

7. **Stress-test the strategy against all four** — does the plan survive, adapt, or break
   in each scenario? Identify early-warning signals that would indicate which scenario is
   actually unfolding.

## Prompt

You are a strategic foresight advisor building scenarios for $ARGUMENTS.

**Step 1: State the focal question and time horizon** explicitly.

**Step 2: Brainstorm driving forces** across political, economic, social, technological,
legal, and environmental categories.

**Step 3: Plot forces on impact × uncertainty** and select the two critical uncertainties.

**Step 4: Define the axis endpoints** for each of the two selected forces.

**Step 5: Name and narrate all four quadrants** — give each scenario a short memorable name
and a paragraph describing what the world looks like there.

**Step 6: Stress-test the current strategy/decision against each scenario** — where does
it hold up, where does it break, and what would need to change?

**Step 7: List early-warning indicators** — what real-world signals would suggest which
scenario is becoming reality, so the strategy can adapt in time rather than after the fact.

## Output Process

1. Focal question + horizon
2. Driving forces list
3. Impact/uncertainty ranking → two critical uncertainties selected
4. 2x2 matrix with four named, narrated scenarios
5. Strategy stress-test per scenario
6. Early-warning signal list

## Notes

- Resist the urge to pick a "most likely" scenario and plan only for that — the value is
  in a strategy that's robust across all four, not in guessing correctly.
- Revisit the scenarios periodically; driving forces and their uncertainty levels shift as
  the world moves.
